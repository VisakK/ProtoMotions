# Lane T in PhysX: the synthesised edges regenerated in the training simulator (2026-10-04)

**Why.** Lane T ([`../t_edges/README.MD`](../t_edges/README.MD)) generated Step 2's edges by MPPI on a MuJoCo copy of
plant v2. That copy matches the masses, gains, torque limits and friction, but not the actuation, the joint limits or
the contacts (PLAN.MD §1.3, "Sim-to-sim"). Before Step 3 splices the edges into release v3, the user asked
(2026-10-04) for the same motions to be generated by MPPI **in IsaacLab/PhysX, the simulator the expert trains in**.
This record is that work: the port, what it measured about the PhysX plant, and the regenerated edges.

Nothing here touched release v2, the MuJoCo runs or any training code. The MuJoCo artefacts in
`output/edge_synthesis/{runs,motions}/` and `../admitted.json` are unchanged; everything PhysX lives in
`output/edge_synthesis/physx/` and [`../admitted_physx.json`](../admitted_physx.json).

## Where it stands

- **The port runs, and it is fast.**
  - 35.5 ms per 30 Hz control step at 1,536 envs, measured alone on the GPU (the stock stepping path: 66–74 ms at
    256–2,048 envs).
  - A recipe with 4 seeds × 512 samples took 23–64 s per second of motion, depending on whether a second IsaacLab
    process shared the GPU. Lane T's MuJoCo took 23–38 s per seed-second on the E-cores.
  - A run repeated with the same seeds and block layout reproduces **bit for bit** (B1, 4 seeds).
- **The PhysX plant is a different plant from the MuJoCo copy** (the next section). The weak point is the right
  arm: it gives way under bent-elbow loads, and lane T's statue feed-forward has no PhysX counterpart.
- **What carries over** (88 variants: 11 recipes × 4 seeds × 2 noise settings):
  - **the quasi-static edges** port well. The slow press **E1** holds the handstand on 4/4 seeds (MuJoCo: 2/5), and
    the mid press on 3/4. The lower **E3** holds crow on 4/4 slow and 3/4 mid.
  - **B1** (crow → plank) lands at 0.7–1.6 BW and holds the plank on 8/8 seeds at both timings.
  - **The two landings into chaturanga do not reach the human's chaturanga.** **E2** (jump-back) lands and holds
    on 3/4 seeds, and **E5** (float-down) on 1/4, but both settle in a *low* chaturanga 0.07–0.09 m (6-body) from
    the exemplar. **MPPI cannot hold the human's chaturanga in PhysX even from rest** (0.11 m), while the G1 expert
    holds it at 0.036 m.
  - **E4** fails statics (as on MuJoCo) and **B2** never reaches crow (as on MuJoCo).
- **Admission (T5, merged record [`../admitted_physx.json`](../admitted_physx.json)).**
  - Under D1's default policy (naturalness and dynamics advisory): **20 variants of 3 edges**, E1 ×7, E3 ×4 and
    B1 ×9. MuJoCo's default set was 13 variants of 5 edges.
  - Under the strict and the naturalness-advisory policies: **0**. Every PhysX execution saturates some joint on
    more than 5 % of steps. This is the training plant's own torque limits, not MuJoCo's.
  - **T5's pending PhysX check now passes for every PhysX clip:** 92 clips load on `smpl_yogi_v2` (plant v1
    refused), stored bodies equal PhysX FK to 1.2e-6 m on 1,840 frames, and **0 of 1,840 resets launch**.
- **Videos:** `output/edge_synthesis/physx/videos/`, one per edge plus the failures, and
  `physx_results_montage.mp4`.


## The PhysX plant is not the MuJoCo plant (measured)

Same exemplars, same gains, PD targets at the pose, open loop (`physx_plant`'s module doc):

| Hold | MuJoCo (`plant_mj`) | PhysX |
|---|---|---|
| Crow -a@651 | root −2.4 cm at 0.17 s, falls at 0.9 s | root −10 cm at 0.2 s; the **right elbow** gives way, saturated at 100 N m |
| Four-Limbed Staff -a@485 (chaturanga) | holds (root 0.265 → 0.219 m) | root 0.265 → 0.14 m; right elbow 0.8 rad off its target (1.25 rad at 10× gains), saturated |
| Handstand -a@1193 | holds 1.8 s | root −10 cm at 0.5 s |

- **It is PhysX's drive, not the asset.** The USD's elbow joints are proper mirrors (identity frames, mirrored
  limits; checked prim by prim). PhysX's spherical joints carry the exp-map coordinates and drive *those*. At large
  rotations (the chaturanga elbow sits at |v| = 1.94 rad, with a large twist component) that is not a restoring
  spring.
- **So the MuJoCo gravity feed-forward does not transfer reliably.** A stiffened PhysX statue does not hold its
  pose: in 67 ms the ×10 statue drifts 0.13–1.0 rad and the ×100 statue (torque limits ×100 too) 0.14–1.28 rad,
  the right elbow worst. Its "torques" are partly a runaway's. Offsets read off it (`pose + τ / kp`, open loop,
  first drop below 10 cm):

  | Pose | Plain | ×10 statue | ×100 statue |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | crow | 0.2 s | 0.9 s | 0.77 s |
  | handstand | 0.5 s | held 1.5 s | 0.47 s |
  | chaturanga | 0.23 s | 0.57 s | 0.9 s |
  | plank | held (7 cm low) | over-lifts 9.5 cm | 0.8 s |
  | tripod | 1.23 s | 0.73 s | 0.47 s |

  It helps some poses, hurts others, and depends on a stiffness that has no right value, so the port optimises
  the start instead (below). Scripts and outputs: `output/edge_synthesis/physx/plant_measurements/`
  (`physx_smoke.py`, `physx_diag.py`, `physx_ff.py`, `physx_prof.py`).
- **Saturation is real, not stop-pressing.** In the MPPI executions, the right elbow's PD demand is about 1.5×
  its 100 N m limit through crow and the lift (E1, B1: 23–31 % of steps). In the jump-back into chaturanga (E2) it
  is about 3.6× the limit on ~100 % of steps, with its twist axis resting on the hard stop. None of it is a drive
  pushing *into* a stop (0 %).
  - In the final handstand the left wrist rests on its 90° stop while its drive pulls back at the limit. That is
    the "hard stops carry load" effect lane T could only note.
  - **MuJoCo's executions of the same edges saturated ≤ 10 % of steps** (worst joint, median over seeds),
    except E5 (14 %) and B2 (52 %, the chest).
- **Hands and the box.**
  - The planted hands drift **0.4–0.7 cm** on the quasi-static edges, against 1.2–2.3 cm in MuJoCo.
  - The box excess is **≤ 1.25°** everywhere: PhysX's stops are hard. MuJoCo's jump-backs and float-downs sat
    10–15° past the box, which is why T4 had to project them.
- **Restores are faithful; the world position is not irrelevant.**
  - A state written into another env continues like the original to 0.09–0.56 mm over 0.3 s, the same as two
    restored copies.
  - The same plan executed from the same restored state in 512 envs at different grid positions spreads by p50
    4–12 mm (max 40 mm on landings) in 0.1 s. That is float32 rounding at 5–100 m, amplified by contact-rich
    dynamics.
  - A whole run is deterministic for a fixed block layout.


## The port: what is lane T's and what changed

**Identical:**
- `edges.json`, the endpoints and the hand-anchor transform;
- the timings and contact schedules;
- the slerp sketches, `--via` and the T2 references in `output/edge_synthesis/quasistatic/`;
- every cost term, scale and weight (the parity test);
- the MPPI algorithm and its settings: 4 knots over 0.8 s, λ 0.05, 2 iterations per replan, replanning every 0.1 s;
- the per-edge recipes, which `queue_edges.sh` copies from lane T's queue scripts;
- `evaluate`'s metric definitions;
- certification (`quasistatic.certify`), the T5 gate, its thresholds, and the 60 fps export instants.

**Changed, each for a measured reason:**
- **No feed-forward; the start is optimised instead** (`--start-iters 16`). Before anything executes, 16 MPPI
  updates of the first plan from the fixed start state, the noise annealed from 3× to 1×.
  - Without it, the crow sagged 12 cm in its first 0.3 s, before 0.1 s replanning could react.
  - With it, every seed starts holding.
- **Noise is per recipe, and PhysX wants more for holds.**
  - The crow hold needs noise 0.12 (lane T: 0.05).
  - 0.12 also rescues the jump-back (E2 1/4 → 3/4 held), but it **breaks the slow reference tracking**: the press
    goes from 4/4 to 0/4.
  - Both passes are kept: pass 1 is lane T's recipes (`*_px`), pass 2 is noise 0.12 everywhere (`*_px12`).
- **Seeds run as blocks:** 4 × 512 samples in one process. A control step costs nearly the same at 256 or 2,048 envs.
- **PD targets are clamped to the range the expert's tanh action can command,** so every executed motion is in the
  policy's action space. The range is ±min(π, 2× the joint's largest limit), ±π for most joints; it rarely binds.
- **During planning the contact sensors are read once per control step** (after its last substep), not every
  substep. The executed motion records every substep (120 Hz), and evaluation and admission read those.
- **Export de-penetrates; it does not project.**
  - T4's projection (`--mode full`) edited the first PhysX B1 run by up to **15.8 cm** (p50 1.3 cm). It was
    enforcing kinematic rows that a dynamic execution does not meet frame by frame.
  - The raw frames met lane T's contract except a 3.2 mm toe-on-toe overlap.
  - The default `depenetrate` mode keeps only the floor, the 253-pair guard and the stay-near terms. Edits are
    ≤ 1.1 cm (p50 0.18 cm), and the contract then passes on every variant.
  - Every export record also carries the raw frames' certification.
- **One Kit trap:** IsaacLab's Kit reads `--verbose` from `sys.argv` and switches to verbose logging (5.7 MB in
  30 s). The driver's flag is `--print-log`.

## What this changes for Step 3

1. **R2 should splice PhysX variants, not MuJoCo ones,** for the edges PhysX executes: E1, E3 and B1.
   - They are the training plant's own trajectories: no projection edits (de-penetration ≤ 1.1 cm), no box excess,
     no reset launch.
   - **The sim-to-sim gap the user deferred is closed for these by construction.**
2. **D1 needs revisiting with these numbers (the user's decision).**
   - Under the default policy the PhysX set is 3 edges. That is the minimum for the paper's "new edges" claim.
   - The jump-back and float-down are out on endpoints, because they end in a low chaturanga.
   - If dynamics becomes hard (it is now the training plant's own saturation), nothing is admitted. Every
     PhysX execution uses some joint at its limit.
3. **T6 is still needed, and it is now cheap.**
