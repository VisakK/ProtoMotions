## 0. Bottom line

1. **The plan attacks the right failure, and Step 2 is its biggest payoff.**
   - The expert replays clips. On ft_a, a far command worked only where some clip demonstrated the passage
     (`expert_revist/hub_swap/far/README.MD`).
   - Graph v2 is a star around standing:
     - 99 of its 196 edges touch standing;
     - among the top-25 nodes, 96 of the 117 two-hop pairs pass only through standing;
     - 180 of 196 edges are seen once;
     - only 2 edges between non-standing holds appear in more than one clip.
   - So at an arm-balance or inversion hold the state has one demonstrated continuation, and the goal is a function of
     the state there.
   - New edges out of those holds are exactly the "same state, different goal" counterfactuals the corpus lacks (§1.3).
2. **Step 1 (AMP) is done. G1 passed every gate but drag, and AMP's effect was posture, not jitter** (2026-10-04;
   record [`g1_amp_eval/README.MD`](g1_amp_eval/README.MD); details in §1.2, "G1 at the end").
   - **What AMP did.** The joint rotations moved toward the human's: 12.6° → 7.3–7.4° median, droop-like bias
     3.2° → 1.0–1.2°, all 23 joints and 55 of 56 clips. A classifier on unseen clips separates policy from human at
     AUROC 0.79–0.81, against 0.991 at e15500. Jitter barely moved, the opposite of what this plan predicted.
   - **Gates** (evaluations 4,000 / 4,500 / 5,000): every group passes (0.971 / 0.932 / 0.986 / 0.964), jerk improves
     weakly, substitutions 5.7 against 6, realisability no worse. **Drag fails** (51.9 J against 39.6 J): back
     upright after a plank-family pose, the policy closes a too-wide stance by sliding a loaded foot.
   - **Clips.** Success 0.923, clips below 0.95 down from 9 to 4. Plow -b now gets up off the floor. Scorpion -b
     holds the hands-only scorpion that gate v2 called beyond the plant. Shoulderstand -a lost its shoulderstand.
   - **The first hard-pose fork from standing**: the supported headstand works at epochs 1,500–4,500 (100 % of
     replicas at 1,500), though not at 5,000.
   - **Attribution.** Only the posture shift and one probe regression (the Pincha frozen hold) start with the AMP
     ramp. **The matched no-AMP control is on hold (user, 2026-10-04)**, so the rest is AMP plus 5,000 more epochs.
   - **Step 3 starts from G1's `epoch_5000.ckpt`** (= `last.ckpt` = `score_based.ckpt`).
   - Design notes that still hold:
     - AMP's value is off the reference: on synthesised edges, whose optimiser artefacts the policy should not copy,
       and in push recovery (PHC used AMP for exactly this);
     - the three corrections to the stock AMP path stay: heading-free features, demonstrations from the x0 clips
       only, and no termination on the discriminator;
     - a co-trained discriminator is not a calibrated naturalness metric, so edge admission keeps its own frozen
       critic (§1.2, card E5).
3. **Step 2 (edges): the census confirms both the edges and the endpoints. Split the generator in two.**

     Staff -a@485, each tracked 1.00 with supports realised 1.00 at e15500.
   - **No clip demonstrates any of them** once support is checked.
   - **Recommended five:**
     - crow → handstand (press);
     - crow → chaturanga (jump-back);
     - handstand → crow (lower);
     - tripod headstand → crow;
     - handstand → chaturanga (float-down).

     Backups: crow → plank, firefly → crow.
   - **The hands stay planted in all five**, so the hand placement is a fixed anchor.
   - **Three are quasi-static strength moves**: the COM stays over the hands throughout. A statics-certified keyframe path
     is the right generator for these.
   - **Two need momentum.** That is where sampling MPC earns its cost, seeded from a kinematic sketch. DynaRetarget's CEM
     over PD knots fails on abrupt contact changes without one.
   - **Measured here:** MPPI in MuJoCo, on the CPU, held crow and the handstand for 5 s on 3 of 3 seeds, at 3.5–4× real
     time. So the generator runs beside GPU training. ProtoMotions' own MuJoCo backend cannot be used: it ignores actuator
     gear, mixes up joint coordinates and runs only one env.

     there also removes the scorpion that gate v2 certified as beyond the plant (§1.3).

   - **What it runs (G3).** A warm start of G1's `epoch_5000.ckpt` on release v3: release v2 plus each admitted
     transition, spliced into a full clip with a real lead-in from its source clip and a real lead-out from its

     - departure anchoring;
     - a per-edge prior in the curriculum;
     - frozen actor and critic normalisers;
     - AMP at G1's weight, synthetic clips never demonstrations;
     - no pushes (G4).
   - **What the planning measured.** The synthesised clips do not meet the real clips at their ends: 4–6 cm at the
     hands on the quasi-static edges, 5–16 cm at the feet on the landing edges. So the generator re-exports with
     exact endpoints before anything is spliced (T6). **Done 2026-10-04:** E1/E3 now meet the clips (the palms

   - **Resume vs warm start.** A resume ignores a new motion library; a warm start works.

   - **Departures are almost never sampled.** Every synthetic transition and every get-up is reached only by uniform
     starts: 0.03–0.07 % of resets land in the first half of an exit. The motion manager needs departure anchoring.
   - **Pushes** exist in the simulator as velocity kicks, but with no curriculum, gating or logging. Pushes pay for
     "outrigger" limbs unless two things are in place:
     - a hands-only guard;
     - an evaluator that sees substitutions (§1.4).

### 1.2 Step 1: the AMP term

**What is right.**
- **AMP is the standard way to keep off-reference behaviour human-like:**
  - AMP (arXiv 2104.02180);
  - PHC (2305.06456), which credits AMP for natural fail-state recovery;
  - a 2026 state-dependent AMP (2605.18611), which gates a separate recovery discriminator by body tilt.

  This project is about to create a great deal of off-reference behaviour: synthesised edges, pushes and recovery.
- **The machinery exists.**
  - `AMP(AMPAgentMixin, PPO)` is at `protomotions/agents/amp/agent.py:22`, with a reusable `AMPTrainingComponent`
    (`component.py`).
  - A warm start from a PPO checkpoint loads with `strict=False` and leaves the discriminator random
    (`component.py:733-763`). This is safe because `setup()` materialises lazy modules first
    (`base_agent/agent.py:215-222`).
  - The evaluator and SequenceViz are untouched, since both read the actor.
- **Estimated cost:** +0.4–0.6 s per epoch (about +5 % on 9.6 s) and about +2.6 GB of GPU memory. Measure it with a
  50-epoch timing run.

**What changes, and why.**
- **Warm start from epoch 15,500, not a fresh 30k-epoch run.**
  - On human clips the tracking reward already defines the motion. AMP's measurable effect will be jitter and the
    occasional odd transition. A 5k-epoch fine-tune shows that as well as a three-day run does.
  - A fresh run costs at least two GPU-days just to reach today's tracking.
  - Pre-register the claim as "no group regresses beyond the noise, jerk falls, the discriminator is healthy", not as
    "AMP improves tracking".
- **Attribution needs a matched control.** The run had not converged (§1.1). The only fair comparison is a
  continuation without AMP from the same checkpoint, about 13 h. Run it while the GPU would otherwise sit idle (§6).
- **The discriminator features must be heading-free.**
  - The stock `historical_max_coords` normalises each frame by `calc_heading_quat_inv` (`envs/obs/humanoid.py:267-305`),
    which projects the root's x axis (`rotations.py:704-713`).
  - That projection is ill-conditioned on 6.1 % of corpus frames, the prone and inverted ones
    (`notes/V9_crucial_investigations/Student_v9_goal_causality_investigation.MD`, `heading_chart_audit.py`).
  - Use instead, per frame (163 values, computed by one shared function on both the agent and the demonstration side):
    - root height;
    - gravity in the root frame;
    - root linear and angular velocity in the root frame;
    - each body's 6D rotation relative to its parent;
    - head, hands and toes in the root frame.
  - Take 8 frames at steps `[1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 14, 20]` (0.67 s at 30 Hz), dense at the start so jitter is visible.
- **Demonstrations come from the x0 clips only, sampled uniformly per frame, without Scorpion -b.**
  - The stock sampler draws through the curriculum's motion weights (`motion_manager.py:320-334`), which change at every
    evaluation.
  - 13.0 % of its frames, and 15.1 % of its 0.67 s windows (28.8 % on the x7s variants), are hold-extension inserts with
    exactly zero velocity. Splices step from 0.21 m/s to 0 in one frame. Her quietest real hold frames still move at
    about 4.5 cm/s.
  - So frozen windows form an unreachable, non-physical cluster. The x0 clips lose nothing, because variant real frames
    duplicate them.
  - Scorpion -b's kick-up is certified beyond the plant.
- **Set `discriminator_reward_threshold = 0`.** Otherwise an early, sharp discriminator ends episodes en masse
  (`component.py:299-338`).
- **Calibrate the weight by advantage scale, not by guess.** Task and style advantages are summed in raw units before
  normalisation (`ppo/agent.py:733-760`, `component.py:389-442`). Tune w so that the style advantage std is 0.2–0.35 of
  the task advantage std. Both are logged (`component.py:713-719`).
- **Watch for reinforced substitutions.**
  - The unconditional discriminator does not know the command: a toe-down crow looks like the corpus's crow entries.
  - Track the substitution count with the fixed evaluator (card E1) against e15500's 10 holds.
  - If it rises, condition the discriminator on the slot-0 contact goal, with mismatched-configuration negatives. That
    is ASE's pattern (`ase/agent.py:382-425`), and `discriminator_step(..., negative_expert_obs)` already supports it
    (`component.py:523-596`).
- **ADD is not a substitute.**
  - `examples/experiments/add/` replaces the hand-weighted tracking sum with a discriminator on (reference − state). On a
    synthesised reference it would track the optimiser's artefacts *more* exactly, the opposite of what Step 3 needs.
  - Its committed example also defines no `reference_obs_components`, so it would not run.
- **On synthesised clips, weight AMP per lineage, and never put synthetic clips in the demonstration set.** Including
  them is circular: the optimiser's artefacts would become "natural".
- **The discriminator is not an admission instrument.**
  - A co-trained discriminator's logits are relative to the current policy and drift during training. On MuJoCo or
    optimiser output they mean nothing.
  - Admission uses a separate frozen critic on the same features (card E5), built as follows:
    - positives: x0 windows and their mirrors;
    - negatives: time-warps of 1.25 / 1.5 / 2×, jitter, kinks, stutter, and linear slerp in-betweens between hold
      exemplars;
    - validation: leave one family out;
    - threshold: the 5th percentile of held-out human windows.
  - Its claim class is "free of these artefact classes", not "human-like". The VLM is not used for naturalness: MLLMs are
    poor judges of human-likeness (Motion Turing Test, arXiv 2603.06181).

**The generator: what the census and the record decide.**
- **The hands stay planted in all five edges.**
  - The hand placement is a fixed anchor, and the problem becomes moving the body over fixed hands, on a known contact
    schedule.
  - The destination exemplar is re-anchored so its hand patches coincide with the source's. That is a rigid yaw/XY
    transform plus a small IK edit if the hand widths differ, re-certified by statics.
- **The reviewer's labels define the optimisation problem.**
  - The ground sets and critical braces at both ends give the phase schedule (labels v2 annotations; `pairs`,
    `pairs_ground`).
  - The sidecar's known-free zones become forbidden contacts during the edge. That forbids, for example, the toe
    kickstand the expert already uses in Eagle.
  - The statics loads give consistency at the ends.
  - This is the paper-worthy link: evidence-gated contact labels specify the trajectory-optimisation problem.
- **Quasi-static edges (1, 3, 4).** Use a statics-certified keyframe path.
  - **Keyframes and in-betweens** come from `retarget_v2`'s machinery: exp-map LM, the joint box as a hard bound, the
    253-pair guard with SAT depth, and the balance margin max(1.5 cm, 25 % of the inradius).
  - **The statics LP runs on every k-th frame.** s* ≤ 1 is the feasibility certificate. A failing frame is a cheap
    negative, found in seconds.
  - **Dynamic check:** MPC tracking in MuJoCo.
  - **The press is the open risk.** Shoulder and wrist torque mid-pike may exceed the plant (the endpoint wrists are at
    s* 0.17 and 0.32). If the LP says so, edge 1 becomes a hop, like Scorpion -b's, or is dropped.
- **Dynamic edges (2, 5, B1).** Use sampling MPC over PD-target spline knots, tracking a kinematic sketch, with the
  contact schedule as cost terms.
  - DynaRetarget (arXiv 2602.06827: CEM over knots 0.25 s apart, 1,024 samples, MuJoCo) needed about 11 min per second of
    motion on 112 cores. Without a dense reference-like cost it failed on abrupt contact changes.
  - So the sketch comes first. If open-loop sampling cannot hold the hands-only phase, the expert policy becomes the
    sampling prior (RGB, arXiv 2606.25123).
- **Sampling MPC on this plant was measured, and it works on the CPU** (24 cores; `evidence/to_bench/`).
  - **Timestep.** MuJoCo plant v2 is stable at dt 1/240 with `implicitfast` (8 substeps per 30 Hz control step). At 1/120,
    noisy controls blew up 35 % of rollouts at σ 0.05 rad and 99.6 % at σ 0.15.
  - **Throughput:** about 23–28k control steps/s on 22 threads. 256 samples × a 1 s horizon take ≈ 0.33 s.
  - **Open loop** with constant PD targets:
    - crow tips at ≈ 1.0 s and the handstand at ≈ 1.4 s, at training gains and at 10× gains alike;
    - chaturanga holds.

    So any planner must replan well inside 1 s.
  - **MPPI holds the poses.** With 4 knots over 0.8 s, N 192, 2 iterations per replan, λ 0.05 and training gains, it held
    crow and the handstand for 5 s on 3 of 3 seeds. That cost 340–420 ms per 0.1 s of motion (3.5–4× slower than real
    time), so a 4 s edge costs 15–20 s per planning pass.
  - Plain predictive sampling held only 3 of 18 runs. MPPI at 10× gains held none.
  - No *transition* has been tried yet.
- **ProtoMotions' own MuJoCo backend cannot be used.** Its defects:
  - one env only (asserted, `mujoco/simulator.py:63`);
  - actuator `gear` ignored, so every joint is bang-bang at its torque limit (`:500-528`);
  - PhysX exp-map `dof_pos` written straight into XYZ hinge `qpos` (`:671`);
  - no ground/pair force split;
  - 50 Hz control.

  So the generator uses its own MuJoCo module. The expert cannot act inside a MuJoCo sampler without a port, which means a
  policy prior needs IsaacLab and GPU time.
- **MuJoCo's XYZ hinge chain is singular near 90°.** Crow's knees (≈ 135°) and hips (−116° to −123°) are past it, so
  MuJoCo's hinge mechanics there differ from PhysX's spherical joints. The crow exemplar is 8.2° past MuJoCo's hinge range
  and the handstand 12.3°. Turn hinge limits off and enforce PhysX's exp-map box as a cost and in the export projection.
- **The MJCF gives robot geoms `condim="1"`,** so self-contact (crow's shin-on-arm brace) is frictionless unless
  overridden.
- **Libraries.**
  - crocoddyl 3.2.1 and aligator 1.1.0 need pinocchio ≥ 3 (the env has 2.7), so they would need a separate venv.
  - mujoco-mpc is not on pip.
  - MJX needs the GPU.

  None is needed: the sampling route is measured to work.
- **Effort** (the benchmark's estimate):
  - the first edge into PhysX, ≈ 35–55 h of work;
  - all five, ≈ 60–90 h.

  With two sessions (quasi-static and dynamic) after a shared plant module, that is about 4–6 days of wall clock.
- **Sim-to-sim.**
  - The MuJoCo trajectory is a *reference*, not a controller. PhysX tracking by the expert is the arbiter (card G2).
  - The record says not to read "MuJoCo holds it" as "PhysX holds it": the IsaacLab statue held none of the poses the
    MuJoCo statue held, and the cause was never found (BUILD_PLAN Step 8).
  - Match what can be matched:
    - friction: PhysX's effective μ is 0.75;
    - torque limits;
    - masses: the MJCF *is* the plant.
  - Judge joint limits in exp-map coordinates, never in MuJoCo's hinge angles.
  - **Closed for E1, E3 and B1 by regenerating in PhysX (2026-10-04, [`t_edges_physx/README.MD`](t_edges_physx/README.MD)).**
    It was deferred to a later step (the user, 2026-10-04), and the user then asked for it the same day. The PhysX
    port measured a larger gap than the list below: the right elbow gives way under bent-elbow loads, and the
    feed-forward does not transfer. The differences lane T measured:
    - MuJoCo chains three hinges per joint, while PhysX uses spherical joints. The PD acts on per-body Euler angles,
      not on exp-map coordinates.
    - PhysX's hard joint stops are absent, so the box is only a cost. Under load the wrists droop past it, up to 16°
      in a jump-back.
    - MuJoCo's contacts are softer.
    - The export projection bridges the box kinematically only (`t_edges/README.MD`, T4).
- **PRISM's lesson** (arXiv 2609.38172, its ablations):
  - admit a synthetic edge only through successful expert rollouts;
  - give the student the expert's rollouts, never the optimiser's kinematics.

### 1.4 Step 3: fine-tuning with the new edges, and perturbations

**What is right.** Full-graph replay is the measured remedy for forgetting in this project:
- the single-clip crow expert beat the 14- and 19-clip trackers;
- adaptive sampling caused the v2 student's regression, and fixed sampling beat it;
- expert60's adaptive sampling collapsed to an effective sample size of 38 of 180 variants (run1 G3);
- ft_a's mixture curriculum fixed that.

**What has to change.**
- **Warm start, not resume.**
  - A resume re-reads the frozen `resolved_configs.pt` and ignores overrides (`train_agent.py:554-577`), so it cannot take
    a new library.
  - `--checkpoint X` under a new experiment name works (`:343-345, 830-836`). Only the weights load; observation sizes do
    not depend on the number of motions or on node ids.
  - The evaluator's EMA scores and the motion weights start fresh (`agent.py:350-371`, `motion_manager.py:441-452`).
- **Normalisers.**
  - Every `RunningMeanStd` is an EMA with decay 0.999 (`agents/common/config.py:36-41`, `normalization.py:152-159`), and it
    re-centres within about 1,000 updates. New clips, and the velocity statistics of pushes, would therefore rescale the
    old clips' inputs.
  - No flag freezes them. The `_freeze_running` attribute exists, but only the PEFT code sets it (`common.py:120,145`).
  - Add a flag, and freeze the actor and critic normalisers in fine-tunes. The epoch-15,500 statistics cover the corpus;
    a hands-only inverted state is not new.
- **Departure anchoring.**
  - The motion manager anchors 60 % of starts at `seg_start − U(0, 0.5 s)`, i.e. at *arrivals*
    (`contact_graph_motion_manager.py:160-194`). 8 % start at t = 0 and 32 % are uniform.
  - A synthetic transition begins where its source hold *ends*. The same is true of every get-up.
  - Measured offline (`evidence/exit_share.py`): the first half of each exit window gets 0.025–0.066 % of all resets,
    about 40–100 starts per 500 epochs.
  - Add `segment_end_prob`: start at `t_end − U(0, pre)`.
- **Up-weighting new clips.**
  - The mixture curriculum gives each motion 0.8/N uniformly plus 0.2 by need (`hold_curriculum.py:340-348`; the
    weights are set at `hold_curriculum_evaluator.py:346-355`). The package's yaml `weight` applies only until the
    first evaluation.
  - Put a per-motion prior into the *uniform* share (`u·w_m/Σw`) and log the effective sample size.
  - `rare_node` reweights segments within a clip, not clips.
- **Pushes are a second phase, after the edges are learnt.** Changing the corpus and the dynamics at once makes either
  failure unattributable.
- **What exists for pushes.**
  - `PushDomainRandomizationConfig` (`protomotions/simulator/base_simulator/config.py:437-474`, wired at `:503-526`)
    applies a whole-body velocity kick on IsaacLab (`isaaclab/simulator.py:1039-1050`). Nothing fires in the first
    `interval_min` after a reset.
  - Past users: `mlp_node_small.py` (0.30 / 0.30 / 0.15 m/s, 0.30 rad/s, every 1.5–4 s), `mlp_edge_v2.py`,
    `mlp_bm_l2c2.py`.
  - The 06k9vjpn run had no randomisation and no reset noise.
  - What is missing: a magnitude curriculum, phase gating and push logging. SequenceViz does not switch pushes off,
    though the evaluator does (`base_evaluator.py:124-134, 300-318`).
- **Outrigger insurance** (`notes/Contact_balance_reward_design.MD` §4.1–4.4).
  - A termination forfeits the bootstrap plus about 1.3 per step. A toe kept down costs 0.04–0.075 per step. So pushes
    pay for the trailing-limb behaviour the release fights.
  - Mitigations:
    - fine-tune only from a checkpoint that already holds;
    - a hands-only guard with automatic magnitude rollback;
    - Bernoulli termination (p ≈ 0.5) on tracking breaches inside push-recovery windows;
    - never restrict pushes to holds;
    - no pushes in evaluation.
  - Gates:
    - ≥ 80 % survival at 0.2 m/s;
    - clean regression ≤ 2 pp;
    - push-attributable terminations < 10 %.
- **Keep the world anchor; do not realign.**
  - Realign is XY root-to-root (`env.py:941-955, 1153-1163`). Per-step realign makes the reference's planted hands and feet
    follow the pelvis, so tracking stops pricing slide. A one-shot realign at the push shifts the planted supports.
  - The literature's two answers, if world-anchored termination kills too many pushed episodes:
    - BeyondMimic (2508.08241): anchor-relative targets, and termination on the anchor's height and orientation;
    - CHIP (2512.14689): shift the *observed* goal by the push in hindsight.
- **Small supports recover by changing contact.** On crow or a handstand, recovery usually means a bail-out (crow →
  squat). Robustness there is a graph property: an edge to a safe node. That is out of scope for the workshop.

### 1.5 Step 4: the student

- **The ceiling is the teacher.** e15500 fails every fork to crow, handstand, Pincha and headstand. A student distilled
  from it would inherit clip replay. So the student is gated on the fine-tuned expert passing:
  - the X0 test at the hubs the new edges create;
  - the fork battery from those hubs.

  **Measured on G3's epoch 3,420 (S0, 2026-10-05; [`s0_x0/README.MD`](s0_x0/README.MD)).**
  - X0 passes at the crow and handstand hubs: 0.31 and 0.24 σ_a above the same-pose floor, against a 0.05
    threshold.
  - From those hubs every new edge and every no-hijack control succeeds on command (1.00).
  - From standing: crow → handstand / plank / chaturanga 0.95–1.00. The handstand-sourced forks fail.
- **What the student adds.**
  - The expert's actor inputs (`mlp_goal_conditioned.py:152-165`) are all deployable in the student's sense. They are
    sim-privileged only against a real robot: per-body contact forces, terrain-grid proximity, and pair-filtered body–body
    forces.
  - The student uniquely provides:
    - **partial goals**: contact-only, or a body subset. The expert needs a full 24-body exemplar in every slot. This is
      the "command a contact configuration, not a pose" story of the paper;
    - a 5-slot window and contact-event history;
    - expert merging;
    - a multimodal head.
- **The port** (`supervised/agent.py:424-452`, `multi_expert.py:161-205`).
  - DAgger labels on student-visited states already work.
  - **The blocker is the expert's goal view.** Expert observations are deep copies of its actor components
    (`expert_utils.py:59-91`), and they read the student's control:
    - the expert was trained on 2 slots × 24 bodies, always visible;
    - the student publishes 5 slots × the 6-body subset, masked.
  - **Fix:** publish an unmasked expert view of the same schedule and rewire the copied components onto it.
  - **A crash to fix first:** `contact_graph_transformer.py:553-559` compares an int with the expert's `future_steps`
    list.
- **"PPO loss", concretely.** Two routes:
  - (a) **Token RLFT with the decoder frozen.**
    - `compute_discrete_latent_ppo_loss` (`agents/common/latent.py:30-84`) and `DiscretePriorPEFTRLFTAgent`
      (`peft/prior_agent.py:98-170`) exist, but only for the GPC prior model.
    - `FSQMaskedMimicModel` needs an adapter with semi-MDP credit assignment: the ratio on refresh rows only, reward
      summed over the variable chunk, discount γ^len.
  - (b) **PRISM/TWIST-style `λ·L_PPO + (1−λ)·L_DAgger`, λ ramped from 0.1 to 0.9.** No code exists for this. On an FSQ
    head it reduces to (a) plus the imitation terms, because the trunk has no action log-probability.
  - **Missing for either route:**
    - a shaped goal reward (contact IoU is tautological under `include_current_segment`);
    - a goal sampler independent of the played clip;
    - goal-referenced termination;
    - a critic.
- **Size:** about 3–4 weeks in all. A Gaussian MLP student with partial-goal inputs and route (b) is the fallback and
  the baseline.

## 2. Facts and traps (verified 2026-10-03)

**Training mechanics**
- **Resume vs warm start.** A resume ignores overrides and a new library (`train_agent.py:554-577`).
  - Warm start: `--checkpoint X`, a new experiment name.
  - `--warm-start-optimization-state` also restores the PPO optimisers, the advantage EMA and the task reward normaliser.
    That is fine with AMP, because the task reward is unchanged; the AMP parts start fresh.
  - Never use it when the *task* reward changes.
- **A loaded checkpoint evaluates right after epoch 0** (`just_loaded_checkpoint_should_evaluate`). That is a free check
  that a warm start loaded: G1's first evaluation matched e15500 within 0.004 on every group.
- **A warm start from an AMP checkpoint (G2 and G3 from G1) loads only part of AMP.**
  - `AMPAgentMixin` loads the discriminator and disc-critic *weights*.
  - `--warm-start-optimization-state` restores only PPO's state. Their optimisers, the AMP reward normaliser and the
    weight calibration start fresh.
  - With its defaults, `run_expert_amp_ft.sh` would again pay w = 0 for 200 epochs and then re-calibrate.
  - For G2 and G3, pass `AMP_START=0 AMP_FULL=0 AMP_CAL_RATIO=0 AMP_W=0.5` (G1's weight). Alternatively, first extend
    `GoalConditionedAMP._load_optimization_state` to call the component's `load_training_state`.
- **Per-lineage AMP weights have no CLI flag yet.** `GoalConditionedAMPAgentConfig.amp_lineage_weights` (stem
  substring → multiplier of the style reward) is wired, but `mlp_goal_conditioned_amp.py` does not expose it. G2 needs
  it (card E6).
  - It applies **per motion**, not per frame (`goal_conditioned.py:196-209`), and it scales the style reward before
    the reward normaliser (`:223-225`). So a spliced clip's real lead-in and lead-out get the synthetic weight too.
- **The AMP launcher's defaults point at G1. This is a trap for G2 and G3.**
  - `run_expert_amp_ft.sh` derives `EXPERIMENT=smpl_yogi_v2_expert56_amp_ft_${SHORT}` and
    `CHECKPOINT=…expert56_${SHORT}/epoch_15500.ckpt` from the release's short hash (`:49-57`).
  - It hard-codes `--segment-start-prob 0.6` (`:164`).
  - On v2 it resumes G1; on v3 it looks for a checkpoint that does not exist.
  - Step 3 has its own launcher (card E6). It has no default experiment name and refuses an existing `last.ckpt`.
- **A resume across motion libraries crashes.** The evaluator's `score_ema` is sized to the library (168 motions in
  v2) and is restored only on a resume (`agent.py:369-371`, `hold_curriculum_evaluator.py:534-537`). The first
  evaluation then raises `IndexError`. A warm start leaves it fresh (`agent.py:373-381`).
- **Synthetic x0 clips would become AMP demonstrations.**
  - `demo_motion_mask` (`goal_conditioned.py:115-119`) keeps every stem that neither matches `_x\d+s$` nor contains
    an excluded substring.
  - Pass `--amp-demo-exclude-motions Scorpion_pose_or_vrischikasana-b SYN_`. The flag *replaces* its default list,
    so Scorpion -b has to be repeated.
- **`eval/perf/score`, and so `score_based.ckpt`, would include the synthetic clips.** So would every `*_x0` total
  (x0 := `variant_s == 0`; `hold_curriculum_evaluator.py:108, 462-470`).
  - Give synthetic clips their own group (`edge`).
  - Read no-forgetting from the human groups and from human-only CSV totals.
- **256-env warm-start smokes skip actor minibatches** (`clip_frac` 0.64–0.77 > 0.6), with or without AMP. The Adam
  state restored from batch 16,384 meets batch-1,024 gradients. At 4,096 envs `clip_frac` is e15500's 0.27. Not a bug.
- **Observation normalisers.** They are EMA 0.999 and keep updating; there is no freeze flag (card E3 adds one).
  - `_freeze_running` is not in the state dict (`common.py:120, 145`), so a freeze must be re-applied on every launch,
    resumes included.
  - The modules are `_actor.mu.norm` and `_critic.norm`. Leave the discriminator's and the disc critic's
    (`models[0].norm`) free.
- **AMP checkpoints add `_discriminator.*`.** A hand-built PPO loader with `strict=True` breaks on them; `pretrained.py`
  reads resolved configs and is fine.
- **The stock AMP demonstration sampler follows the curriculum** (`motion_manager.py:320-334`). Override it (card E2).
- **The heading chart is ill-conditioned on 6.1 % of frames.** This affects any feature built with
  `calc_heading_quat_inv`.
- **Inserted hold frames have exactly zero velocity**, and the splices step to zero in one frame. Keep them out of anything
  that learns what human motion looks like.

**The simulator and the plant**
- **Pushes.**
  - On IsaacLab a push is a root-velocity write.
  - The evaluator switches pushes off; SequenceViz does not (card E4).
  - There is no post-push grace, only the reset grace of 5 steps (`base_env/utils.py:65-68`).
- **Realign moves planted supports with the pelvis.** Do not use it for push recovery.
- **The plant.**
  - PhysX `dof_pos` is the exp-map with *hard* box limits.
  - The MJCF's hinges are XYZ Euler angles, so never cite MuJoCo hinge violations as the plant's.
  - Effective ground friction in PhysX is 0.75.
  - All 253 non-parent–child body pairs collide.
- **MuJoCo for the generator** (`evidence/to_bench/`).
  - Use dt 1/240 with `implicitfast`; 1/120 blows up under sampling noise.
  - Reset actuator `gear` to 1.
  - Floor friction 0.75 and robot 0.5, since MuJoCo combines by max.
  - condim 3 on the robot geoms: the MJCF's condim 1 makes braces frictionless.
  - Hinge limits off: the XYZ hinge chain is singular near 90°, where the crow's knees and hips sit.
  - ProtoMotions' MuJoCo backend is not usable for this (§1.3).
  - **Measured by T1 (2026-10-03; `t_edges/README.MD`):**
    - **Elliptic friction cones.** MuJoCo's default pyramid is a diamond: a plant box slides at tan 0.70 on the
      diagonal (μ 0.53). Elliptic sticks at 0.74 and slides at 0.76 in every direction.
    - **Per-body hinge orders** (`plant_mj.HINGE_ORDER`). The MJCF's x-y-z order puts y, the largest range of the
      hips, knees, ankles and spine, in the singular middle slot. With each body's smallest-range axis in the middle,
      the MPPI handstand hold went from 2 of 5 seeds to 3 of 3.
    - **Hinge limits cannot stand in for PhysX's box.** Per-body Euler angles differ from the exp-map components by
      tens of degrees; 14,505 of 81,512 corpus frames would hit a false limit. The box stays a cost, and T4's export
      projects into it.
    - **PhysX's hard stops carry load that MuJoCo's free hinges cannot.** Under load the wrists droop past the box,
      up to 16° in a jump-back.
    - **Tracking a quasi-static reference needs a gravity feed-forward** (`edge_mppi.FeedForward`). Without it the
      press lagged and toppled (29 cm mean error); with it, 3.3 cm.
- **Plant v2's limits and gains.**
  - Torque limits: hip 300, knee 200, ankle 100, toe 20; torso 300, spine 200, chest 150; neck 75, head 25; thorax 200,
    shoulder 150, elbow 100, wrist 30, hand 15 N·m.
  - Training Kp/Kd: leg 800/80, toe 500/50, spine 1000/100, neck 1158/116, arm and head 500/50, wrist and hand 300/30.
  - At their holds the wrists bind first: s* is 0.165 for crow, 0.322 for the handstand and 0.259 for chaturanga.
- **Statues.**
  - A statue at training gains buckles (use 10× stiffness).
  - An arm-balance statue tips without feedback. So open-loop trajectories on hands-only balances are ill-conditioned:
    MPC must replan, or carry a feedback prior.
  - The IsaacLab statue harness is broken (D3); do not use it.

**Sampling and the curriculum**
- **Anchoring targets arrivals only.** `rare_node` reweights segments inside a clip, not clips.
- **The package yaml `weight` is used only until the first evaluation.** After that the mixture curriculum decides.
- **The panel commands each plan to many replicas:** 341 on the expert's 12-plan panel at 4,096 envs, about 36 on the
  student panels.
  - Score all of them, and use per-goal hold metrics (`best_pose_err` is a minimum over time).
  - A single plan's rate has moved by up to 0.22 between panel runs.
