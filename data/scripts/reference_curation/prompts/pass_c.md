Two versions of one moment of a recorded yoga motion are shown as simulated avatars, A and B. Review them from the images alone.

Read packet.json first (the legend and the colour key), then every image it lists (img_1.png, img_2.png, ...) with the Read tool. In each image the left column is avatar A and the right column is avatar B, every pair of panels seen from one camera; each panel carries its tag at the top left and its avatar letter at the top right. The translucent grey body is the human performer at the same moment, the same in both columns. One version may be an edited copy of the other. The order is random: do not assume either one is the original.

Answer:

1. same_pose: do A and B show the same pose performed the same way, with the same body parts on the floor, the same limbs raised and the same overall shape? A small change of height, a limb a few centimetres lower, or a hand or foot turned flat onto the floor is still the same pose. A different pose, or a limb in a clearly different place, is not. Give a short reason.
2. closer_to_human: which avatar lies closer to the grey body, above all where the grey body meets the floor.
3. more_natural: which looks more like a real person in this pose.
4. artefacts: every defect you can see in either avatar: a part below the floor (through_floor), a part passing into another part (through_body), a joint turned the wrong way or further than a body can turn (impossible_joint), a limb pulled apart or detached (pulled_apart), a jerk between the rows of the time strip (jitter), anything else (other). Name the avatar, the part and the panels, and mark it major if it changes how the pose is done, minor otherwise.
5. floating_parts: for each avatar, the parts that rest on the floor in the grey body but hover visibly above the floor in the avatar. The coloured drop lines show such a gap.
6. summary: one or two sentences.

Use equal when neither avatar is closer or more natural, and cannot_tell when the images do not let you decide. Do not name the pose.
